# Note only. No "reject:" line, so this repeat stays in every mean and figure.

Suspected tool slip in the gripper during the set-up press.

This repeat removed 0.00 deg of alignment error where r01 and r02 removed
1.56 and 1.62 deg under an identical configuration. The before value was also
1.7 deg lower than theirs, so the trial started from a different first-contact
angle as well.

The declared mount play is a rotation about Y_EE, and the commanded twist puts
Y_EE 25 deg from t2. On a t2 excitation the slip axis therefore nearly
coincides with the axis being corrected, so the contact moment can be taken up
by the tool turning inside the gripper instead of the arm turning. Alignment is
inferred from the EE pose, so that is indistinguishable from no correction.

Kept deliberately. The slip is a real property of this fixture rather than a
recording fault, and excluding it would remove the clearest evidence the
campaign has that the limitation exists. It is reported in the thesis instead.

Consequence for the result: a slip can only hide correction that physically
happened, so it biases a t2 condition downward and never upward. The
t2-corrects-more-than-t1 asymmetry is a lower bound.
